Eating Color

The first thing I heard about was anman, a sweet red-bean bun.

When Hime was a child, if there were nikuman, a steamed meat bun, and anman to choose from, she would end up picking the anman, she says.

“But I liked the taste of nikuman better.”

Then why anman?

“Because it had a pink mark on it.”

...The one on top?

“Yeah. Taste-wise, I liked nikuman better. But I’d end up picking the anman anyway.”

What was that about?

She would choose anman even though she didn’t like the taste, just because it had a pink mark on it.

Well, maybe kids do things like that.

Or so I thought. It hadn’t stopped when she grew up.

This time, it was macarons.

A deep, bright, intense pink.

Hime ate that macaron again and again.

Was it that good?

“Not really.”

What?

“If it had been a different color, I think I would’ve eaten it once and that would’ve been it.”

This was weird.

Apparently, it wasn’t about the taste.

The same thing happened with a donut covered in red icing, she said.

“That one was like, I have to eat it!”

You have to?

“Of course.”

Because it was red?

“Yeah.”

Oh, right. Food isn’t just about taste. You enjoy how it looks and the colors too.

“No!”

No?

“I’m literally eating the color!”

There she went saying something strange again.

But Hime didn’t look like she thought she’d said anything strange.

As I listened, I realized that ever since she was little, this had been especially strong with foods that weren’t normally that color.

Green pasta.

Something that would normally be pale was green instead.

The whole bun could be pink, even if it was the kind of dough used for nikuman.

The shell of a cream puff could be green.

Tsukimi dango made it even clearer.

“If there’s even one yellow one in the pack, I’ll buy it.”

Just one?

“Yeah.”

The rest are white, right?

“I don’t want the normal-colored ones.”

“I could throw away all the white ones.”

That far, huh.

It didn’t seem like she was particularly attached to the dango.

By this point, you want to think she’s drawn to things that are a color they normally wouldn’t be.

All right. Got it.

Or so you think.

But then the macaron gets in the way.

Macarons don’t have a standard color to begin with.

They can be pink, yellow, or green.

Even so, that deep, bright, intense pink was special.

So it wasn't just because the color was unusual for that food.

But it wasn't about the taste either.

And it wasn't because it looked pretty.

“That’s what I’ve been telling you.”

Right.

Hime had been saying the same thing from the beginning.

“Sometimes, I eat color.”

I was the one trying to turn it into something I could understand.

Still, there was one thing I didn’t understand.

Why did she eat color?

“Dunno why I eat by color, though.”

You don’t know?

She had been eating that way for a long time without knowing why.

It was later, while we were talking about something else.

Hime suddenly said,

“Nourishment.”

Nourishment?

Stronger than liking something.

Something necessary.

Something to live on.

After talking about that, we came back to color.

We also talked about whether color might be some kind of sign.

But Hime stopped that one too.

“Ugh. No.”

If it was a sign, then there had to be something beyond it.

It would mean she was seeing the color and receiving something else from it.

But that wasn’t it.

“The color itself is nourishment.”

And that was where it stopped.

A pink mark.

Red icing.

Yellow dango.

Deep, bright, intense pink.

She had been eating color all along.

She had been saying exactly that from the beginning.
